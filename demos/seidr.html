Seiðr

In Norse cosmology, seiðr was a form of magic practiced by the völva — the wandering shaman-seeress. Through ritual trance induced by sacred plants, drumming, and chant, she could travel between the nine worlds of Yggdrasil and return with visions of fate.


The fly agaric — Amanita muscaria — was known to shamanic cultures across the circumpolar north. Its consumption broke the veil between worlds, burning runes into the retina of the inner eye.

Völva

The völva carried a staff (völr) and wore ritual garments adorned with amber, bone runes, and the pelts of animal spirits. She was both feared and revered — capable of seeing the threads of wyrd woven by the Norns beneath Yggdrasil's roots.


When the mushroom opened her inner eye, the runes did not need to be read. They appeared burning behind the retina, carved into the very fabric of sight itself.
